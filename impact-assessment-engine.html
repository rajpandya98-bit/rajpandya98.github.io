Change-Control Impact Assessment Engine

Rule-Based Decision Support & Weighted Confidence Scoring

Process Automation Excel / VBA Decision Logic GMP Workflow

The Problem

At a GMP-regulated manufacturing site, every change control requires an Impact Assessment (IA) — a reviewer has to determine which of roughly 17 departments (Validation, Engineering, Quality Control, IT, Health & Safety, and others) need to review a proposed change before it can move forward.

This judgment call was made case by case, from memory and experience, with no structured reference point. It was slow, and two reviewers could reasonably reach different conclusions on the same case.

What I Built

A rule-based decision-support tool designed to mirror how an experienced reviewer actually thinks through a case, rather than just matching keywords:

  • Case-family classification — the change description is matched against a defined taxonomy of case types (facility/utility changes, IT and system access, lab and analytical methods, packaging and labeling, safety and environmental risk, and others), each with documented reasoning for why specific departments are typically involved.
  • Weighted scoring engine — for each department, the tool combines three signals: category match strength, rule-based trigger phrases specific to that department's scope, and similarity to precedent from a library of historical, completed cases.
  • Negative rules layer — a deliberate set of exclusion rules that suppress false positives. For example, the word “workflow” alone shouldn't pull in Quality Control if the surrounding context is clearly a packaging record update, not a lab issue. This was the part that took the most iteration to get right.
  • Confidence output — each department receives a total score, a confidence percentage, and a clear recommendation (Include / Review / Exclude), displayed on a dashboard alongside a plain-language explanation of why.

I used AI assistance to help translate expert human reasoning — the actual mental steps a reviewer takes — into this structured rule logic, then built the scoring model and automation in Excel and VBA.

How a Case Flows Through the Engine

Step What Happens
1. Intake Reviewer enters the change-control number, title, and description into a single intake field.
2. Classification The engine matches the description against case families and department-specific trigger phrases.
3. Scoring Each department accumulates a weighted score from category match, rule match, and historical precedent.
4. Exclusion check Negative rules suppress likely false positives before a final score is set.
5. Recommendation Dashboard displays Include / Review / Exclude per department, with the reasoning stated in plain language.

Why I Built It

Nobody assigned this. I noticed reviewers were re-deriving the same judgment calls, case after case, often reaching similar conclusions through similar reasoning — which meant that reasoning could be captured and made consistent. I treated it as a product problem: what does the reviewer actually need to know, in what order, to make a fast and defensible call?
